A Letter For You

Hi Mr. Husband,
Happy Birthday. Yes, we are still in a long‑distance relationship and you are reading this message when I am asleep or on a call. I wish I could be with you — not just today but every day — but God and the Canada Government have other plans. I remember in the last letter I wrote before marriage, I said that if I ever had the chance to use a time machine, I wouldn’t change anything in our story. I would always choose you. But I broke that promise many times. I regretted our marriage sometimes — when I couldn’t handle all the major changes at once, when people gave me a hard time, when you were being an asshole, when I missed my job, Bangalore, my friends, my independent single life. I’m not very proud of it, and I’m not ashamed of it either. I know you also have moments like this.

“Happily ever after” or being in love all the time is a lie. Humans are not capable of that. No one can love another person 100% all the time. Sometimes I’ll be at 40% and you’ll be at 60%. Sometimes you’ll be at 25% and I’ll be at 75%. Some days both of us can’t love each other enough. It sounds unpleasant, but those days will eventually pass, and we will be okay again. We will still be those cringey couples who hug over video calls.

So even if I chose the time machine and made you vanish from my life and memory, I know I would wait for you — just like I did before — and when you came into my life again, I would fall in love with you just like I did before. So if I am at my 1%, be my 99%. Because even if I say things like “I regret you” or “I regret this marriage,” and sometimes even mean it in the moment — it’s only out of anger or hurt. I never wanted to feel that way. The truth is, you are the only person who makes me feel at home, and I seriously don’t know what I would do without you.

We promised to be with each other in sickness and in health, but I really want to know if you can do one thing: Will you be with me when I am wrong? Will you be with me when the whole world is against me?

I will be with you when you are fighting the world. Damn, sometimes I will fight with you so hard that you’ll feel fighting the world is much easier.

With all that said… Happy Birthday, Mr. Husband — my dear karadi, eruma, madu, loose pakki. I miss you. I love you.

From your wife, who is doing a crappy job at being a wife.
